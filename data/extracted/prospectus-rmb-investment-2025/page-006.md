# A History of Growth

Rwanda's mining story dates back nearly a century. Since the 1920s, geological surveys have revealed substantial deposits of tin, tantalum, tungsten (the 3Ts), and gold across the country. These deposits are closely linked to steep faulting and folds associated with intrusive granite cupolas, creating a unique geological setting rich in mineral potential.

The country hosts numerous historical mineral occurrences and operational mines. Major commodities include:

*   **Cassiterite** - Tin with grades of up to 76% Sn.
*   **Wolframite** - Tungsten with grades ranging from 35% to 70%.
*   **Columbite-tantalite (Coltan)** - Tantalum grades between 25% and 55%.
*   **Native gold** - Exceptionally pure at 99.999% Au.
*   **Pegmatite-related rare minerals** - including rare earth elements (REEs), Beryl, Lithium, Phosphate minerals, and a variety of gemstones.

Some quarry resources include Granite, Quartzite, Volcanic Rocks, Clay, Sand, and Gravel.
The country also possesses extensive peat reserves, which are harnessed for electricity generation and as an alternative to firewood, underscoring Rwanda's broader natural resource wealth.
